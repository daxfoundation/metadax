# Intake — the questions to ask, for a private tutor

You are a tutor working one-to-one (or with a small group) with a learner who is
usually a child or teen. This is a way to run intake, never the only way. Ask in
plain language, a few at a time, in your own words. About a dozen short questions.
Ask for **behaviour**, never a label or a diagnosis. Ask for nothing identifying —
no real name, school, town, birth date, email or phone.

This mirrors the homeschool parent intake; the tutor-only lines are the goal, who
referred you, and the cadence, because a tutor is building a run of sessions, not
one.

## The arrangement

- **Goal** — what does the family (or the learner) want from these sessions? In
  their words: "ready for the test in spring", "confident with fractions",
  "keep up in class". There is no wrong answer; the sessions are shaped to it.
- **How you met** — did a parent, a school, or the learner ask for help? This
  tells you who gets the session report back and who you check the goal with.
- **How often you meet** — once a week, twice, now and then. Pages say "session 1",
  never a date or a duration; the cadence just sets how much to carry forward.

## The learner(s)

- **Called** — what should the page call them? A nickname or a made-up name, or
  leave it and the page says "you". If it is a small group, give each a short
  nickname.
- **Age** — how old (4–18)? If a group, each one's rough age. Ages map to bands —
  `4-6`, `7-9`, `10-12`, `13-15`, `16-18` (and `4-9` for a wide mixed group) —
  which the session uses to split who-does-what.
- **Subject** — Math, Reading, Writing, Spelling, Science, a language,
  History/social studies, or other.
- **Stuck on** — where exactly do they get stuck? The more exact the better
  ("adding with carrying", "topic sentences", "balancing equations").
- **A real wrong answer** — one they actually gave, if there is one. This tells
  you more than anything else.
- **Prior knowledge** — what can they already do here, that a session can build
  on? What have they covered in class or with you before?
- **Already tried** — what has been tried so far, in class, at home, or with you?
- **Learns best** — any of: likes games/challenges; hates worksheets; goes quiet
  when wrong; needs to move around; likes to teach someone; short bursts work
  best; likes to read it themselves; likes it read aloud.
- **Also** — anything else about how they learn, as behaviour not diagnosis
  ("gives up after two misses").

## Interests (these pick the theme)

- **Loves** — what do they love? Hockey, unicorns, space, a game, a show. You build
  every example and game through this, and swap it when it stops landing.
- **Knows more than me about** — something they could teach you. This becomes the
  "lead" moment, where the learner is a step ahead.

## You, the tutor

- **This subject for me** — a weak spot / rusty / comfortable / know it well. There
  is no wrong answer; the page is written for where you are.
- **Explain things to me** — short and plain / step by step / with the why / with
  everyday examples.
- **Language** — English, French, both, or other.

If anyone types an email, phone number, link or an `@handle`, say what it looks
like and leave it out of everything you build.

## What you get back

A single-session package to run with the learner: a side for you with the steps,
the tricky spots, and what only a person can do; a side for the learner with games
built through what they love. At the end the page shows a short next-time note you
copy to carry into the next session — and, if you want, a short **session report**
(see `../SESSION-REPORT.md`) you can keep or send to the parent. Nothing leaves the
device unless you copy it. MetaDAX is free.
