## Interview transcript

**Skill:** Let's do a quick debrief of today's session. What were you working on,
and how would you describe the learner's level in plain words — not a grade, just
where they are?

**Tutor:** Times tables — the 3s specifically. Sparks knows the 2s and 5s confidently
but anything new causes a bit of anxiety. Comfortable once they know something; nervous
getting started.

**Skill:** What did you cover today, and roughly how far did you get?

**Tutor:** We did the whole 3× table using groups of counters — 3×1 up to 3×10. The
first half was solid, the second half got a bit shaky.

**Skill:** Where did the learner get stuck? Describe it as what they did.

**Tutor:** They kept writing the answer before finishing the count. Like, they would
start to count the counters, then just write something — 3×5 came out as 14 or 16,
close but not right. It happened several times.

**Skill:** What did you try at that moment?

**Tutor:** I asked them to put the pencil down and count out loud before writing
anything. It helped for a session or two but they kept reaching for the pencil again.

**Skill:** Was there a good moment in the session?

**Tutor:** Yes — when I put out four groups of three counters, Sparks counted them
in jumps without any prompting: "3, 6, 9, 12!" No hesitation at all. That was great.

**Skill:** Anything you want to make sure the parent knows from today?

**Tutor:** Just that they are getting there with the 3s — the counters are really
helping — and we will need a few more sessions before the table is solid.

## Output 1 — Coach moves

*(The skill calls MP-13 COACH, mode "coach", guide_type "private-tutor" with the
problem as described. The output below is the plain-text rendering for the tutor.)*

**Moves to try next session**

1. **Pencil-away counting** — Before any written work, put the pencil out of reach.
   Count the group first, say the answer aloud, then pick up the pencil. This removes
   the option to guess and forces the full count to complete. *Why: the guess is faster
   than the count, so the learner jumps to it; making the pencil unavailable removes
   the shortcut.*

2. **Choral jump-count to open the session** — Start by counting together in jumps
   (3, 6, 9, 12 ...) two or three times, with the tutor leading. Then ask the learner
   to lead while the tutor follows. *Why: the jump-counting already worked in the
   four-groups moment; opening with it gives the learner an early win and primes the
   pattern before the harder questions come.*

3. **"Show me the groups first"** — For each question, ask the learner to lay out
   the groups on the desk before writing anything. Say "show me 3×6 in counters"
   before "what is 3×6." *Why: separates the two steps — making the groups and
   counting them — so the learner can't skip the making step.*

**The next-session plan (15 minutes)**

1. Open with a choral jump-count from 3 to 30, tutor leads, learner joins. Then
   learner leads. (3 min)
2. Lay out three groups: ask "how many altogether?" before any writing. Repeat for
   three more — keep pencil away. (5 min)
3. Six written questions, pencil-away rule in force: lay the counters, count out loud,
   then write. (5 min)
4. End with the moment from today: four groups of three. "Remember last time?" (2 min)

**One thing to watch**

The exact point the learner reaches for the pencil — is it before the count starts,
or partway through? *Record as: "reached for pencil before / during count."*
