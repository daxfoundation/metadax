# How to use MetaDAX — a guide for parents

**Make your first lesson in about ten minutes.**

You teach your own child. MetaDAX helps you build them one lesson for the exact
spot where they are stuck, through the thing they love. This is the
plain-language help: pick a route, run it, open what it builds, and go. It is a
way in for one child today — a way, never the way.

## Your first lesson, start to finish

1. **Get it, by whichever route suits you.** The Claude skill, a plain prompt for
   any AI chat, or a web questionnaire if you would rather not use an AI at all.
   Pick one below.
2. **Answer its questions.** About a dozen, in plain words — a nickname (never a
   real name), a rough age, the subject, the exact spot where it keeps going
   wrong, and what your child loves. Nothing identifying.
3. **Open what it builds.** You get a two-page package: a page for you and a page
   for your child, with a game built through what they love. Open it in the
   player, or print it.
4. **Run the session, then come back.** The page leaves a short note for next
   time; the next session is built straight from it.

## A few things to know first

- It is free, and it stays free. It runs on an AI subscription you already pay
  for, or on the web questionnaire, which costs nothing.
- It describes what your child *does*. It never names a condition or a diagnosis,
  on any page, ever.
- Your child's side is a game and a set of answered questions, used with you
  beside them. There is no AI on your child's side.
- Nothing is stored about you or your child unless you choose to share a generic
  version, and a person reviews that before it ever appears.
