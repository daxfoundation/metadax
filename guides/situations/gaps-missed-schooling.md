# Gaps from missed schooling -- holes from lessons, terms or years that were missed

Meta DAX guides > Situations > Gaps from missed schooling

> **This is a situation, not a label.** Describe what you see and when it happens, not what the learner "is". If someone has mentioned a condition, that is for a professional to assess -- here we work only from what is observable.

## What you might see
- Handles some material confidently but then hits a wall at an apparently unrelated point
- Uses a workaround or invented method that is almost right but breaks down in certain cases
- Has no memory of being taught a particular foundational step that peers seem to know
- Asks a question that reveals a gap several levels below the current topic
- Gets frustrated when peers move quickly through something that was never covered
- Decodes the words in a problem but does not recognise the underlying operation or form
- Shows uneven knowledge across a subject -- strong in some areas, absent in others -- without a clear pattern

## What's often going on
Missed schooling creates specific, identifiable gaps -- not a general capability deficit. A learner who missed a term of fractions may be completely solid on everything else and simply have no memory of that content because they were never there for it. The gap can be invisible for a while if later learning can work around it, then suddenly visible when the missing piece becomes essential. The learner is not behind in any global sense; they are missing a specific thing, and that thing can usually be found and filled once it is identified. Shame about the gap is often the bigger obstacle.

## First moves
1. Find the specific gap before trying to fill it -- do a short, low-pressure diagnostic by asking the learner to show you how they would start a problem in the suspect area. Watch where they stop or guess.
2. Name what is happening plainly and without judgment: "it looks like this topic was not covered when you were in school -- that is fine, we can cover it now."
3. Treat the missing content as new learning, not remediation -- present it at the same pace and with the same materials you would use for any new topic.
4. Do not work backward through everything -- identify the minimum prerequisite needed for the current task and fill that one thing first.

## By guide type
- **Parent or caregiver:** Ask the learner what they remember learning and what feels unfamiliar -- they often know where the hole is better than anyone.
- **Private tutor:** Keep a short topic map of what is confirmed secure, what is confirmed missing, and what is not yet tested -- update it each session.
- **Classroom teacher:** Offer a quiet, individual catch-up task rather than singling the learner out -- discretion matters when gaps are present.
- **Peer or older sibling:** Frame it as "I learned this a different way, let me show you" -- peer catch-up is most effective when it does not feel like remediation.
- **Coach or mentor:** Draw a clear line between what the learner has not been taught yet and what the learner is now learning -- the first is circumstance, the second is progress.
- **Elder or grandparent:** Tell stories of your own interrupted learning or late learning if you have them -- it normalises gaps across a lifetime.
- **Workplace trainer:** Identify the specific on-the-job skill that is blocked by the gap and address only that skill -- stay focused and practical.
- **Volunteer teacher:** Build in a brief review of prerequisites at the start of each session; it serves everyone and singles no one out.

## A 10-minute activity
**The prerequisite probe**

1. (2 min) Choose one topic where the learner struggles. Write three problems: one that requires only the prerequisite, one that requires both the prerequisite and the current topic, and one that requires only the current topic.
2. (3 min) Ask the learner to attempt all three in any order without help. Do not comment while they work.
3. (3 min) Look at the results together. If the prerequisite problem was the one that blocked them, you have found the gap. Name it clearly: "this is the piece we are going to cover next."
4. (2 min) Write one sentence together describing the gap as a learning target, not a deficiency: "We are going to learn [x] because it unlocks [y]." Keep this visible.

## What not to do
- Do not assume a gap means the learner is weak generally -- missing one topic is not a statement about anything except what was not taught.
- Do not make the catch-up work feel separate or lesser -- present it as just another part of learning, which it is.
- Do not start filling without first finding the exact missing piece -- guessing the gap and teaching the wrong thing wastes time and adds confusion.
- Do not revisit the reason the schooling was missed unless the learner raises it -- the reason is not relevant to the learning; the gap is.

## What to watch, and record
- The learner identifies a gap themselves before you do -> record as: "learner self-identified gap in [topic] -- good self-awareness, build on it"
- The learner fills the missing piece and the related difficulty resolves quickly -> record as: "gap filled: [topic] -- downstream difficulty cleared"
- The learner has multiple connected gaps suggesting a longer missed period -> record as: "possible extended absence period -- map broader topic sequence before proceeding"
- The learner resists working on the gap material directly -> record as: "sensitivity around [topic] gap -- use indirect or embedded approach"
