# Rushes and Makes Careless Errors -- knows it, but races and slips on the easy parts

Meta DAX guides > Situations > Rushes and Makes Careless Errors

> **This is a situation, not a label.** Describe what you see and when it happens, not what the learner "is". If someone has mentioned a condition, that is for a professional to assess -- here we work only from what is observable.

## What you might see
- Finishes tasks noticeably faster than peers, then has more errors
- Makes mistakes on steps the learner demonstrably knows how to do
- When asked to check work, misses the same errors or checks too quickly
- Sighs, crosses out, or shows frustration at their own mistakes afterwards
- Accuracy improves markedly when a timer or check-step is built into the task
- Skips reading all the way to the end of a question before starting
- Makes more errors late in a session than at the start

## What's often going on
Speed can feel like competence -- finishing first can be a source of pride or relief,
especially if earlier tasks were hard. The learner may also have learned to rush because
slow, careful work has historically felt unrewarding or drawn unwanted attention.
Alternatively, attention may dip once the "hard part" feels solved, so the final
checking steps get very little processing. This is a pacing and habit issue, not a
knowledge gap.

## First moves
1. Separate speed from success explicitly: "Fast is great; accurate and fast is the goal."
2. Introduce a physical pause before submitting -- a finger-check, read-back, or a
   two-breath rule. Name the step so the learner owns it.
3. Reduce the number of items per session and raise the quality bar. Ten careful answers
   beat twenty rushed ones.
4. Let the learner self-mark immediately and count errors. Seeing the pattern themselves
   is more powerful than being told.

## By guide type
- **Parent or caregiver:** Celebrate a careful finish over a fast one at home; avoid
  timing practice unless accuracy is already solid.
- **Private tutor:** Build a "stop-and-check" ritual at the end of every task and make
  it non-negotiable but brief (30 seconds).
- **Classroom teacher:** Assign a self-check column on worksheets so checking is a
  visible, gradable step.
- **Peer or older sibling:** Take turns: one person does, the other reads the question
  back aloud before the answer is written.
- **Coach or mentor:** Frame accuracy as professional habit: "people who are good at
  this always double-check the easy parts."
- **Elder or grandparent:** Play slow-and-careful games (cards, puzzles) alongside the
  learner to model a different pace without lecturing.
- **Workplace trainer:** Build a checklist into the task itself; rushing costs rework
  time -- make that visible with real examples.
- **Volunteer teacher:** Pair the learner with a slightly slower partner and give marks
  for the pair's combined accuracy.

## A 10-minute activity
**Two-Pass Practice**

1. (3 min) Give 5 short problems. The learner works at their normal pace, no comment.
2. (2 min) Before any marking, the learner reads each question and their answer aloud,
   touching each word or number with a pencil.
3. (2 min) The learner marks their own work and counts errors on each pass.
4. (3 min) Discuss: "Where did pass two catch something pass one missed?" Write the
   count. Repeat next session to show the trend improving.

## What not to do
- Do not say "slow down" as the only instruction -- it is vague and feels like criticism.
- Do not reward finishing first, even informally ("oh, you're done already!").
- Do not add more problems as a consequence of finishing fast -- that punishes speed
  without teaching accuracy.
- Do not check the work for the learner; they need to own the checking habit.

## What to watch, and record
- Error rate on first pass vs. second pass -> record as: "self-check saved N errors"
- Which step type produces the most careless errors -> record as: "slips on: [step]"
- Whether error rate rises in the second half of a session -> record as: "fatigue
  pattern: errors rise after [N] minutes"
- Whether verbal read-back catches errors that visual scan missed -> record as:
  "read-back works / visual scan misses"
