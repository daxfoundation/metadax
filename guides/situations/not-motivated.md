# Not Motivated by the Subject -- does not care about this subject (yet)

Meta DAX guides > Situations > Not Motivated by the Subject

> **This is a situation, not a label.** Describe what you see and when it happens, not what the learner "is". If someone has mentioned a condition, that is for a professional to assess -- here we work only from what is observable.

## What you might see
- Does the minimum needed to get through, then stops
- Asks "when will I ever use this?" or "why do we have to do this?"
- Engages well when talking about topics they chose; disengages on assigned ones
- Speeds through to finish, but with little investment in quality
- Physically turns away, doodles, or finds other things to do during the subject
- Completes the task correctly when pushed but shows no curiosity beyond the task
- Mood and energy visibly shift when the subject changes to something they care about

## What's often going on
Motivation is not a fixed quantity -- it is a response to perceived relevance,
perceived competence, and the degree of choice in the situation. A learner who sees no
connection between this subject and anything they care about has a reasonable, rational
response to avoid it. It is also possible the subject has a history of feeling too hard
or too easy, making engagement feel pointless either way. The guide's job is not to
force enthusiasm, but to find a genuine thread between the learner's own interests and
the material.

## First moves
1. Ask -- without an agenda -- what the learner is genuinely interested in or good at.
   Listen. Do not jump straight to "well, that's connected to maths because..."
2. Find one real, honest connection between their interest and the subject. If you
   cannot find one, be honest and work on a different angle (usefulness, choice, or
   short-term gain).
3. Offer a small, real choice within the task: which problems to do first, which topic
   to explore, which format to present in. Autonomy increases engagement.
4. Lower the stakes for one session -- explore the subject together with curiosity
   rather than assessment. See what surfaces.

## By guide type
- **Parent or caregiver:** Avoid framing the subject as something that must be endured;
  look for it in real life and point it out without pressure.
- **Private tutor:** Ask the learner directly: "What would make this session not a
  waste of your time?" Take the answer seriously.
- **Classroom teacher:** Let learners choose one aspect of a project -- the topic,
  the medium, the question -- to create a personal foothold.
- **Peer or older sibling:** Connect the subject to something you both care about;
  shared context makes the material feel less alien.
- **Coach or mentor:** Link the skill to something the learner is already working
  toward in their own life or work.
- **Elder or grandparent:** Share how the subject shows up in a story from your own
  life -- a real use, not a hypothetical one.
- **Workplace trainer:** Anchor every skill to a specific task the learner will
  actually perform; skip content that genuinely does not apply to their role.
- **Volunteer teacher:** Start with what the learner wants to achieve (a goal they
  named), then work backwards to the skill needed to get there.

## A 10-minute activity
**The Interest Bridge**

1. (3 min) Ask the learner to describe something they genuinely enjoy or are good at --
   a hobby, a game, a skill. Let them talk without steering. Take brief notes.
2. (3 min) Together, look at one concept from the subject. Ask: "Does any part of what
   you just described connect to this, even loosely?" Stay curious. If they find one,
   they own it. If not, that is honest too -- move to the next step.
3. (2 min) Reframe the session goal around something concrete and near-term: "By the
   end of this session, you'll be able to do X" -- where X is small and real.
4. (2 min) At the end, ask: "Was any part of that less bad than you expected?" That is
   enough for now. Do not oversell. Record any genuine moment of interest.

## What not to do
- Do not try to talk the learner into enthusiasm -- it does not work and damages trust.
- Do not punish or shame the lack of interest; it is information, not defiance.
- Do not pretend the subject is inherently exciting if the learner clearly disagrees --
  find a different angle rather than insisting on one that is not landing.
- Do not remove all choice in the name of structure; that removes one of the few levers
  available.

## What to watch, and record
- Topics or formats that produce even brief engagement -> record as: "partial
  engagement: [topic/format] on [date]"
- Whether learner-chosen goals lead to better completion -> record as: "learner goal:
  [X] -- completed: yes / partly / no"
- Moments where the learner asks a question unprompted -> record as: "spontaneous
  question on: [topic]"
- Whether stated irrelevance decreases as context is made clearer -> record as:
  "'why do I need this?' said: [frequency] this session"
