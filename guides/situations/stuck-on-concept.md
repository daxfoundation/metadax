# Stuck on one concept -- keeps hitting the same wall on one idea while the rest moves fine

Meta DAX guides > Situations > Stuck on one concept

> **This is a situation, not a label.** Describe what you see and when it happens, not what the learner "is". If someone has mentioned a condition, that is for a professional to assess -- here we work only from what is observable.

## What you might see
- Gets the right answer on similar problems but stumbles on this exact type every time
- Explains surrounding ideas clearly, then goes quiet or guesses when this one comes up
- Tries the same approach repeatedly even after it has failed
- Asks to skip or move on as soon as this concept appears
- Shows frustration, sighs, or says "I just don't get this one" without explaining further
- Copies a worked example without being able to apply it a step later
- Makes progress, then resets to zero at the next session as if it were new again

## What's often going on
The learner may be missing one small piece of knowledge that sits underneath this concept -- a prerequisite they never fully secured. Alternatively, the explanation they received may have used a model or analogy that does not map onto how they currently think about the topic, so every new attempt rebuilds on a shaky foundation. A third possibility is that this concept requires holding several things in mind at once, and the load is just slightly beyond what feels comfortable right now, so the learner defaults to mimicking rather than understanding.

## First moves
1. Back up one level and check the prerequisite -- ask the learner to explain the step just before this one. If that is also shaky, go back one more.
2. Switch the representation -- if you have used symbols or rules, try a physical or drawn version. If you have used words, try a diagram or a concrete example from daily life.
3. Ask the learner to teach it back to you, even incorrectly. Where they hesitate or misspeak shows you exactly where the gap is.
4. Reduce the problem to its smallest possible case -- strip away all extra steps until only the core idea remains, then rebuild slowly.

## By guide type
- **Parent or caregiver:** Do a quick kitchen or walk-around version of the idea -- everyday objects often unlock what a worksheet cannot.
- **Private tutor:** Spend the first five minutes of the next session finding the exact prerequisite gap before touching the concept again.
- **Classroom teacher:** Assign a short pair-share where another learner explains their thinking aloud -- peer language often lands differently than teacher language.
- **Peer or older sibling:** Say "let me show you how I think about it" rather than re-explaining the rule -- your personal shortcut may be the missing link.
- **Coach or mentor:** Connect the concept to something the learner already does well in practice or in life; use that bridge first.
- **Elder or grandparent:** Slow right down and try story form -- "imagine you have a pile of stones and you want to..."
- **Workplace trainer:** Find a real task at work where this concept appears and let the learner try it in context before returning to the abstract version.
- **Volunteer teacher:** Keep a short note of which representations you have already tried so you do not repeat them next session.

## A 10-minute activity
**The smallest true case**

1. (2 min) Ask the learner to pick the simplest version of the problem they can imagine -- one number, one word, one object. Write it down together.
2. (3 min) Solve only that version, out loud, step by step. Ask the learner to stop you the moment something feels wrong or unclear.
3. (3 min) Add one layer of complexity. Repeat. Ask the learner to predict what will happen before you do it.
4. (2 min) Ask: "What is the one thing that made the simple version work?" Write the answer in the learner's own words and keep it visible for next time.

## What not to do
- Do not repeat the same explanation louder or faster -- if it did not land the first time, volume and pace will not fix it.
- Do not move on and hope it resolves itself -- small gaps compound quickly and the learner will hit this wall again.
- Do not express surprise that this specific thing is hard -- it signals that the difficulty is unusual, which adds pressure.
- Do not over-scaffold by doing it for them -- guided struggle is more useful than a smooth demonstration they cannot replicate.

## What to watch, and record
- The learner explains a prerequisite correctly without prompting -> record as: "prerequisite secure: [name the prerequisite]"
- The learner applies the concept correctly using a non-standard representation -> record as: "unlocked via [representation used]"
- The learner hesitates at a specific sub-step every time -> record as: "consistent stall at: [describe the sub-step]"
- The learner applies it correctly in one session but not the next -> record as: "retention gap -- revisit with spaced repetition"
