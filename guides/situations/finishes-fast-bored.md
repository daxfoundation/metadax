# Finishes fast and gets bored -- races through, then has nothing to do and may disrupt

Meta DAX guides > Situations > Finishes fast and gets bored

> **This is a situation, not a label.** Describe what you see and when it happens, not what the learner "is". If someone has mentioned a condition, that is for a professional to assess -- here we work only from what is observable.

## What you might see
- Completes set work in a fraction of the expected time
- Begins talking to others, fidgeting, or wandering when work is done
- Produces correct answers but with minimal working or explanation shown
- Gets visibly restless during whole-group instruction on material they already know
- Asks "is that it?" or "what do I do now?" moments after beginning
- May rush and make careless errors specifically because the pace feels too slow
- Shows much more engagement when tasks are open-ended or self-directed

## What's often going on
Fast completion usually means the task is pitched below where the learner currently is -- the material is not new enough to hold their attention. Finishing quickly is not the same as understanding deeply, though, and it is worth checking whether the learner can explain, apply, or extend their answer before assuming full mastery. In some cases the learner has learned to move fast to reach the end point and avoid the harder thinking that comes with staying in a task -- the speed is a strategy, not evidence of genuine fluency. In others, the work genuinely is too easy and needs replacing or extending.

## First moves
1. Before adding more work, ask one "why" or "how do you know" question -- this tests whether the fast answer reflects genuine understanding.
2. Offer a harder version of the same task rather than more of the same type -- depth before breadth.
3. Invite the learner to create a question on the same topic that would challenge a classmate or sibling. Authoring is harder than answering.
4. Have a standing extension task ready -- something open-ended that cannot be finished quickly and does not feel like a punishment for being done.

## By guide type
- **Parent or caregiver:** Keep a small collection of "what if" questions for topics the learner covers at school -- pull one out when work is done early.
- **Private tutor:** Redesign the session so the first task is already pitched at the right level -- do not start below and try to extend upward.
- **Classroom teacher:** Use a task menu where everyone works to the same outcome but can enter at different levels of complexity.
- **Peer or older sibling:** Ask the learner to explain their method to you as if you are learning it for the first time -- teaching is generative.
- **Coach or mentor:** Channel the energy: give the learner a small research or planning role that serves the group and uses the surplus time well.
- **Elder or grandparent:** Invite a story connection -- "now that you have done that, can you think of a time in real life where that would matter?"
- **Workplace trainer:** Assign a stretch observation task alongside the core learning -- "while you are waiting, notice how [x] is handled at the next station."
- **Volunteer teacher:** Keep a bank of extension prompts on index cards that the learner can pick themselves -- self-direction reduces the waiting feeling.

## A 10-minute activity
**The question author**

1. (2 min) The learner finishes the set task. Ask them to look back over it and pick the step they found most interesting or most surprising.
2. (3 min) Ask them to write one question -- the hardest question they can think of -- based on that step. It should be a question they are not sure how to answer themselves.
3. (3 min) Together, explore their question. You do not need to answer it fully -- the exploration is the activity. Ask the learner what they think and why.
4. (2 min) The learner writes one sentence about what they would need to find out or learn to answer their own question. This becomes a thread for next time.

## What not to do
- Do not give more of the same work as a default -- volume without depth is not more useful for a learner who has already grasped the concept.
- Do not frame extension tasks as a consequence of being fast -- "since you are done, do this extra work" feels like a penalty.
- Do not ignore the behaviour and hope they will self-manage quietly -- unstructured waiting time tends to generate disruption.
- Do not assume fast always means correct -- check quality before calibrating the next task level.

## What to watch, and record
- The learner's extension answer shows genuine depth beyond the original task -> record as: "ready for higher-complexity task in [topic area]"
- The learner rushes and makes errors they would not normally make -> record as: "pace-driven errors -- task may be too low; check with one harder version"
- The learner engages longer when the task is open-ended -> record as: "sustained attention with open tasks -- use more open formats"
- The learner creates a question that goes beyond the current topic -> record as: "self-generated stretch question: [note the question or theme]"
