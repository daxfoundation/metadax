# Intake — the questions to ask, for a workplace trainer (L&D)

You are training adults at work — onboarding, a tool or process, a compliance
topic, a skill for a team or cohort. This is a way to run intake, never the only
way. Answer in plain language, in your own words. About a dozen short questions.
Describe the learners by **role and behaviour**, never a label or a diagnosis, and
never by name. Ask for nothing identifying — no real names, no personnel data, no
email or phone. There is no child here, so there is no child-safety framing; the
learners are adults.

## The training and the goal

- **Goal** — what should people be able to do after? In your words: "run the new
  returns process without a cheat sheet", "spot a phishing email", "close a ticket
  to standard."
- **Who the learners are** — their role or roles ("new hires in support", "floor
  staff", "managers"), not names. Roughly how many in the cohort?
- **Why now** — a new tool, a new rule, a recurring mistake, a yearly requirement?
  This sets the tone and how much it leans on the source material.
- **How often you meet** — a single session, a short series, a rolling cohort.
  Pages say "session 1", never a date; the cadence sets how much carries forward.
- **How it runs** — a room with a screen, people on their own devices, printed
  handouts, or self-paced? This shapes what the page assumes.

## What people are learning

- **Stuck on** — where do people get it wrong or slow down today? The more exact
  the better ("they skip the approval step", "they confuse the two refund types").
- **A common mistake** — one you see a lot. This tells you more than anything else,
  and it names a step, never a person.
- **Prior knowledge** — what can the cohort already do that this builds on? What is
  assumed from their role or earlier training?
- **The spread** — a mix of experienced and brand-new? Describe it as behaviour
  ("the veterans tune out the basics"), never as labels.
- **Source material** — the policy, the SOP, the manual, the slide deck, the
  standard this must match. Paste or name it; the session is grounded in it, so it
  teaches your process, not a generic one. Leave out anything identifying.
- **What must be exactly right** — any step, number, or rule that cannot be
  paraphrased loosely (a compliance line, a safety step). These are kept verbatim.
- **Already tried** — how has this been trained before, and where did it lose
  people or fail to stick?

## Interests (these pick the theme)

- **What lands with this cohort** — the scenes skew to real work: a worked
  example, a process walk-through, a before/after, a realistic ticket or case.
  Name the kind of example that feels real to them. The theme is swapped if it
  stops landing.
- **Who can lead** — is there a step some of the cohort already do well and could
  walk the rest through? This becomes the "lead" moment, where a learner is a step
  ahead.

## You, the trainer

- **This topic for me** — a weak spot / rusty / comfortable / know it well. The
  page is written for where you are.
- **Explain things to me** — short and plain / step by step / with the why / with
  everyday examples.
- **Language** — English, French, both, or other.

If anything typed in looks like an email, phone number, link or an `@handle`, say
what it looks like and leave it out of everything you build.

## What you get back

A session package to run with the cohort: a side for you with the steps, the
tricky spots, the source grounding, and what only a trainer can do; a learner side
with practice built on realistic cases from your material. At the end the page
shows a short next-time note you copy to carry into the next session, and a session
report (see `../SESSION-REPORT.md`) you can keep as a record of what was covered.
Nothing leaves the device unless you copy it. MetaDAX is free.
