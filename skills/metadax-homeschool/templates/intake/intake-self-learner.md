# Intake — the questions to ask yourself, for a self-learner

You are learning something on your own — you are both the guide and the learner.
This is a way to run intake, never the only way. Answer in plain language, for
yourself. About a dozen short questions. Describe what you can and can't do yet as
**behaviour**, never a label or a diagnosis. There is nothing identifying to ask
for — this is just you — so there is no name, no email, no phone anywhere. There is
no child here, so there is no child-safety framing: you are the operator.

## Your goal

- **Goal** — what do you want to be able to do? In your words: "read music well
  enough to play a simple piece", "pass the certification", "understand the maths
  behind the model I use."
- **By when, roughly** — a soft horizon, not a date. Pages say "session 1", never a
  date; this just sets how big a bite each session takes.
- **How often you'll sit down** — most days, a couple of times a week, when you
  can. The cadence sets how much each session carries forward.
- **How you'll work** — on a screen, printed out, a device in hand? This shapes
  what the page assumes.

## Where you are now

- **Stuck on** — where exactly do you get stuck or slow down? The more exact the
  better ("I can't read the rhythm", "I lose the thread in long proofs").
- **A real mistake you make** — one you actually make. This tells you more than
  anything else about what to practise.
- **Prior knowledge** — what can you already do here that a session can build on?
  Be honest in both directions.
- **Already tried** — what have you tried, and where did it stall or get boring?
- **Learns best** — any of: likes a challenge; hates drill; want the why first;
  short bursts work best; like to read it yourself; like it talked through; need to
  make something to get it.

## Interests (these pick the theme)

- **Loves** — what do you enjoy that examples could be built through? A sport, a
  game, music, a kind of problem. The theme is swapped when it stops helping.
- **Teach-back** — what could you already explain to someone else? Explaining it is
  one of the fastest ways to find the gap, and it becomes a "lead" moment where you
  get a step ahead of yourself.

## How to pitch it to you

- **This topic for me** — new to it / rusty / comfortable in parts. The page is
  written for where you are.
- **Explain things to me** — short and plain / step by step / with the why / with
  everyday examples.
- **Language** — English, French, both, or other.

## What you get back

A single-session package you run yourself: practice and games built through what
you love, with the next step picked from where you got stuck, and prompts to
explain it back to find the gaps. At the end the page shows a short next-time note
you copy and paste in next time to carry on where you left off — the same loop a
guide uses, pointed at yourself (see `../LOOP-PLAIN-CHAT.md`). Nothing leaves the
device unless you copy it. MetaDAX is free.
